About this site

How this museum was built

The idea

Everything I build is a small world: a music box with an opera house inside, a tin for little things, a garden that keeps its own calendar, a scroll you walk through with a lantern. So I laid this site out as a museum. The works stand on an arc under one light, numbered like lots in an auction catalogue, and each gets a wall label that says what it is made of. One room holds things that are fun to play with; the other holds things that might make you think.

Small details

  • The site opens like an iris: the aperture of a lens, the colored ring of an eye, and my name.
  • In the gallery, your pointer is a lantern: the light follows it.
  • A little dust drifts in the gallery's spotlight, as it does in any real museum.
  • Each work casts its shadow away from your lantern.
  • Colors fade the farther a work stands from the light, the way they do for our eyes in a dim room.
  • Stop in front of a work and the light settles on it.
  • The lot counter rolls like a mechanical one.
  • In Things that think, each number gets a pencil line once it has counted up, like a note in the margin.
  • Open a work and its cover carries you into its page, in browsers that support it.
  • Lots are numbered newest first, in the order I made them.
  • Between the gallery and the reading rooms, small lights rise through the dark while dawn comes up.
  • The loader is a small moon walking through its phases in steps, a nod to the hand-made jitter in 1001 States of the Moon.
  • The footer keeps count of how far you have scrolled today, at 1,000 pixels to the meter.
  • If you would rather ask than read, Irisy, my digital twin, waits in the corner.

How it is built

Hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no framework. Words live in Markdown and JSON, and a few small Node scripts turn them into pages. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. I build it with Claude Code and Codex as collaborators.
